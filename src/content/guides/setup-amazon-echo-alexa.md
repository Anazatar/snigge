An Amazon Echo is a smart speaker or display that responds to voice
commands using Amazon's Alexa service. You can ask Alexa questions, set
timers, hear weather updates, play audio, and control compatible
smart-home devices.

Setting up an Echo is usually straightforward. You will need the Echo
device, its power adapter, a phone or tablet with the Alexa app, an
Amazon account, and your home Wi-Fi name and password.

The exact screens can vary by Echo model and app version, but the main
steps are similar.

## 1. Get your Echo and phone ready

Place your Echo on a stable surface near a power outlet. For the first
setup, it is helpful to keep it within a reasonable distance of your
Wi-Fi router.

On your phone or tablet, install the official **Amazon Alexa** app from
the Apple App Store or Google Play Store. If you already have it, check
for an update before continuing.

Make sure you can sign in to your Amazon account. You will also need
your home Wi-Fi network name and password.

> **Before you begin:** Use the Amazon account you want associated with
> the Echo. Keep your account password private, and do not share it with
> anyone who is helping you set up the device.

## 2. Plug in the Echo

Connect the power adapter supplied with your Echo to the device, then
plug the adapter into a wall outlet. Use the power adapter made for your
model.

Give the Echo a moment to start. Many Echo speakers show a rotating
orange light when they are ready for setup. The exact light pattern can
vary by model.

If you have an Echo Show or another Echo device with a screen, follow
the instructions displayed on the screen. It may ask you to choose a
language, connect to Wi-Fi, and sign in.

## 3. Add the Echo in the Alexa app

For most Echo speakers without a screen, use the Alexa app to complete
setup.

1.  Open the **Amazon Alexa** app on your phone.
2.  Sign in with your Amazon account if asked.
3.  Tap **Devices**.
4.  Tap the **+** button.
5.  Choose **Add Device**.
6.  Select **Amazon Echo**.
7.  Choose the Echo model or the general Echo option shown.
8.  Follow the instructions in the app.

The app will search for your Echo and guide you through the connection.
If your device does not appear automatically, make sure it is powered on
and in setup mode.

> **Screenshot suggestion:** Show the Alexa app's **Devices** screen and
> the **Add Device** option. Use a demonstration account with personal
> information hidden.

## 4. Connect the Echo to your Wi-Fi network

During setup, the Alexa app will ask you to choose a Wi-Fi network.
Select your home network and enter its password carefully.

Your Echo needs an internet connection to answer most questions and use
online services. If your router has separate 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz network
names, choose a network supported by your Echo model and follow the
app's instructions.

Wait while the app connects the Echo. This may take a few minutes. When
setup is complete, the app should confirm that the device is ready.

If the Echo asks you to update its software, allow the update to finish
before unplugging it.

## 5. Choose a name and location for your Echo

If the app offers the option, give your Echo a name that helps you
recognize it, such as **Kitchen Echo** or **Bedroom Echo**. This is
especially useful if you have more than one Alexa device.

You may also be able to assign it to a room. Room names help when you
later manage multiple devices or create smart-home groups.

You can usually change device details later in the Alexa app. Open
**Devices**, select your Echo, and look for its settings.

## 6. Try a few simple Alexa commands

Once your Echo is connected, try saying the wake word, which is usually
"Alexa," followed by a request. The wake word is the name that tells the
device you are speaking to it.

For example, you can try:

-   "Alexa, what time is it?"
-   "Alexa, set a timer for five minutes."
-   "Alexa, what is the weather today?"
-   "Alexa, tell me a joke."

Speak naturally and wait for the device to respond. If Alexa does not
hear you, move a little closer and make sure the microphone is not
muted.

Some requests depend on your region, account settings, or linked
services. You may need to set up a music service separately before
asking Alexa to play from it.

## 7. Understand the microphone and privacy controls

Echo devices have a microphone control that lets you turn the
microphones off. On many Echo speakers, this is a button with a
microphone symbol and a line through it. When the microphone is off,
Alexa cannot hear the wake word until you turn it back on.

You can also review Alexa privacy options in the Alexa app. Look for the
privacy section in the app's settings to review voice history and
available controls. Menu names may vary as Amazon updates the app.

If your Echo has a camera, such as some Echo Show models, check the
model's camera controls and privacy options too. You can use the
physical camera shutter if your model includes one.

> **Privacy tip:** Place the Echo somewhere you are comfortable having a
> voice assistant. Learn where the microphone-off button is so you can
> use it whenever you want more privacy.

## 8. Connect compatible smart-home devices (optional)

If you have a compatible smart bulb, plug, or other smart-home product,
you may be able to control it with Alexa. Check the product packaging or
manufacturer's information for compatibility.

To add a device, open the Alexa app and select **Devices**. Tap the
**+** button, choose **Add Device**, select the type or brand, and
follow the instructions.

Some products require their own app or an additional setup step before
Alexa can find them. Only connect devices you recognize and are
authorized to manage.

## Troubleshooting: If Alexa will not set up

**The Echo does not show an orange light.** Wait a little longer after
plugging it in. If it still does not enter setup mode, check the model's
instructions. You may need to restart it or use its setup control.

**The Alexa app cannot find the Echo.** Check that Bluetooth and the
required phone permissions are enabled, keep your phone near the Echo,
and make sure the Echo is in setup mode. Close and reopen the Alexa app
if necessary.

**The Echo cannot connect to Wi-Fi.** Check that your Wi-Fi password is
correct and that other devices can connect to the network. Move the Echo
closer to the router and try again.

**You cannot sign in to Amazon.** Confirm that the email address or
phone number is correct. If you cannot access your account, use Amazon's
official account-recovery process rather than sharing your password with
someone else.

**Alexa does not respond to your voice.** Check that the microphone is
not muted. Speak clearly from nearby and make sure the Echo is not
covered by objects.

**The app's menus look different.** Amazon updates the Alexa app
periodically. Look for **Devices**, the **+** button, and **Add
Device**, or use Amazon's official setup instructions for your exact
Echo model.

## Your Echo is ready to use

Once your Echo is connected to Wi-Fi and registered in the Alexa app,
you can start with simple questions, timers, and reminders. You can
explore more features gradually rather than changing lots of settings at
once.

Keep the Alexa app updated, learn how to mute the microphone, and check
the privacy settings so you understand how your device works in your
home.
