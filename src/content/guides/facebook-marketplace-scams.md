Facebook Marketplace can be a handy place to find second-hand furniture,
electronics, household items, and local bargains. But not every listing
or message is genuine. Knowing how Facebook Marketplace scams work can
help you avoid losing money, sharing personal information, or handing
over an item to someone who has not paid.

Scams can target buyers and sellers alike. A fake seller may take
payment and disappear, while a dishonest buyer may send a false payment
notice or ask for a refund that is not really owed. The safest approach
is to slow down, check the details, and use payment and collection
arrangements you understand.

## Step 1: Recognize the most common Facebook Marketplace scams

Scammers use several familiar tricks. The details may change, but the
aim is usually to get your money, your item, or access to your accounts.

Watch for these warning signs:

1.  **A listing that looks too good to be true:** An expensive phone,
    computer, or appliance is advertised at an unusually low price. The
    seller may claim there are lots of interested buyers and demand a
    deposit to reserve it.
2.  **A seller who does not have the item:** The photos may be copied
    from another website, or the seller may avoid answering specific
    questions and refuse to let you inspect the item.
3.  **A fake payment message:** A buyer sends a screenshot or email that
    appears to show payment, but no money has arrived in your account.
4.  **An overpayment story:** A buyer claims to have paid too much and
    asks you to send back the difference. The original payment may be
    fake, reversed, or made with a fraudulent method.
5.  **A request to pay or communicate elsewhere:** Someone insists on
    moving to text, email, another messaging app, or an unfamiliar
    payment website.
6.  **A request for a verification code:** A buyer or seller says they
    need a code sent to your phone to prove you are genuine. That code
    may actually give them access to one of your accounts.

## Step 2: Check the seller, buyer, and listing

A profile or listing can look convincing without being genuine. Take a
few minutes to check whether the details make sense before agreeing to a
transaction.

1.  Open the person's Facebook profile and look for signs of normal,
    consistent activity. A new profile or very little history is not
    proof of a scam, but it is a reason to be cautious.
2.  Read the listing carefully. Check the description, condition,
    location, and whether the price is realistic for the item.
3.  Look closely at the photos. If they seem unusually polished or
    inconsistent, search for the same image online to see whether it
    appears in unrelated listings.
4.  Ask a specific question about the item's condition or features. Be
    wary if the answer is vague or does not match the listing.
5.  For an expensive item, ask to see it in person and inspect it before
    paying.

Do not rely on a profile photo, friendly conversation, or
positive-sounding claims as proof of identity. Scammers can use copied
photos and accounts that appear established.

## Step 3: Pay safely when you are buying

Payment arrangements matter. Some Marketplace purchases may offer
checkout and purchase protection, while local pickup and
person-to-person payments may not. Availability and eligibility vary by
country, listing, and transaction type, so check the current terms shown
by Facebook before relying on any protection.

1.  Read the payment and purchase terms for the specific listing before
    you pay.
2.  Where eligible checkout is offered, use the payment process shown
    within Facebook rather than a link sent by the seller.
3.  Avoid paying a stranger by gift card, cryptocurrency, wire transfer,
    or an unfamiliar payment service.
4.  Do not send a deposit just because the seller says someone else is
    waiting.
5.  For a local purchase, arrange to inspect the item before completing
    payment whenever possible.

Facebook explains that eligible purchases made through its checkout may
qualify for Purchase Protection, but local pickup transactions arranged
directly between people are generally not covered in the same way. Read
Meta's current [Marketplace buying and selling
guidance](https://www.facebook.com/help/marketplace/) and the terms for
your location before making a decision.

## Step 4: Avoid fake payments when you are selling

Sellers are often targeted with fake payment confirmations and
overpayment stories. A screenshot, text, or email from the buyer is not
proof that the money has reached you.

1.  Agree on the price and collection or delivery arrangements in the
    Marketplace conversation.
2.  Check your own bank account or payment app directly. Do not use a
    link or phone number sent by the buyer.
3.  Do not hand over or ship the item until the payment is confirmed in
    your account.
4.  Never refund an alleged overpayment based only on a screenshot,
    email, or pending notification.
5.  If a buyer says you must pay a fee or upgrade an account to receive
    money, stop the transaction and check directly with your payment
    provider.

A scammer may send a fake payment confirmation that looks official. They
may also claim the payment is delayed, then pressure you to release the
item anyway. Wait until you can independently confirm the funds have
arrived and are available.

## Step 5: Arrange local collection carefully

Meeting in person can let you inspect an item before buying, but make
sensible arrangements. Avoid sharing more personal information than the
transaction requires.

1.  Choose a busy, public, well-lit meeting place.
2.  Arrange to meet during daylight where possible.
3.  Tell someone you trust where you are going and when you expect to be
    back.
4.  For a valuable or bulky item, consider bringing another person.
5.  Inspect the item before paying, and make sure it matches the
    description.
6.  Do not feel pressured to complete the sale if something seems wrong.

For large items that must be collected from a home, think carefully
about privacy and safety. You do not need to invite a stranger inside.
If the arrangement makes you uncomfortable, cancel and look for another
buyer or seller.

## Step 6: Keep your account and personal information private

Some Marketplace scams are designed to take over Facebook accounts or
collect information that can be used elsewhere.

-   Keep the conversation on Facebook while you are arranging the
    transaction.
-   Do not share your Facebook password, email password, or one-time
    verification codes.
-   Be cautious of links claiming you must sign in again to receive a
    payment or confirm a delivery.
-   Open Facebook and payment services through their official apps or by
    typing their web addresses yourself.
-   Do not provide copies of identity documents or unnecessary personal
    details to a stranger.
-   Turn on two-factor authentication for your Facebook account.

If a message claims to come from Meta or Facebook support, do not trust
it just because it uses a logo. Check account notifications and security
information inside Facebook itself.

## Step 7: Report a suspicious listing or account

If you think a listing or person is trying to scam you, stop
communicating and report the activity through Facebook.

1.  Open the Marketplace listing or the person's profile.
2.  Look for the **Options** menu or the reporting control. Its position
    and wording may vary by app version.
3.  Choose the option to report the listing, seller, or buyer.
4.  Follow the on-screen instructions and select the reason that best
    describes the problem.
5.  Keep copies of relevant messages and payment records.

You can also use Meta's [scam and safety
guidance](https://www.meta.com/safety/topics/safety-basics/tools/avoid/)
to review current advice.

If you have lost money, contact your bank or payment provider
immediately. In the US, you can report fraud to the [Federal Trade
Commission](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). In the UK, report it through
[Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/); in Scotland, contact
Police Scotland on 101.

## Troubleshooting: What if you think you have been scammed?

**I paid, but the seller has stopped replying. What should I do?**

Contact your bank or payment provider straight away and explain what
happened. Ask whether the payment can be stopped, recalled, or disputed.
Save the listing, messages, and payment details, and report the account
to Facebook.

**The buyer sent me a payment screenshot. Is that enough?**

No. Open your own bank or payment app and check whether the money has
actually arrived. Do not hand over or ship the item based on a
screenshot or email.

**Someone says they need my phone number to send a verification code.
Should I give it to them?**

Do not share verification codes. A stranger does not need your account
security code to buy an item. End the conversation if they keep asking.

**The seller wants a deposit before I can see the item. Is that safe?**

It is risky, especially if the seller is pressuring you or will not let
you inspect the item. Do not send money you cannot afford to lose. Look
for another listing if the arrangement does not feel right.

## Buy and sell with confidence

Facebook Marketplace can be useful, but take your time with every
transaction. Check the listing, keep communication on the platform,
verify payments yourself, and never share passwords or verification
codes. If a buyer or seller rushes you or asks for an unusual payment,
it is safer to walk away.
