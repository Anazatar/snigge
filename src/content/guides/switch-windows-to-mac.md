Switching from Windows to Mac can feel unfamiliar at first. The desktop
looks different, some buttons have new names, and even simple tasks such
as finding a file or closing a window may work a little differently.

The good news is that you do not need to learn everything at once. Your
Mac can do many of the same things as a Windows PC, and once you know
where the familiar features are, everyday tasks become much easier.

## What you'll need

-   Your Mac and your Windows PC, if you plan to move files directly
    between them.
-   Your Wi-Fi password and the passwords for important accounts.
-   An external drive or cloud storage if you want to copy files
    manually.
-   A little time to get comfortable with the new layout.

## Step 1: Get to know the Mac desktop

The Mac desktop is the main screen you see after signing in. It has a
few important areas that replace familiar parts of the Windows desktop.

1.  Look at the **menu bar** along the top of the screen. The menus
    change depending on which app you are using.
2.  Find the **Dock**, usually along the bottom of the screen. It holds
    shortcuts to apps and shows which apps are open.
3.  Click the **Finder** icon in the Dock. Finder is the Mac app for
    browsing files and folders, much like File Explorer in Windows.
4.  Open a folder in Finder to see its contents.
5.  To open an app, click its icon in the Dock, or open **Launchpad** if
    it is available on your version of macOS.

The Apple menu is at the top-left corner of the screen. Click it to find
options such as **System Settings**, **Restart**, **Shut Down**, and
**About This Mac**.

Apple menu\]

> **Good to know:** On a Mac, the menu bar belongs to the app you are
> currently using. It is not attached to each window in the same way as
> many Windows app menus.

## Step 2: Learn the Mac keyboard differences

The keyboard is one of the first differences you may notice. Mac
keyboards use a **Command** key where many Windows instructions use the
**Ctrl** key.

1.  Find the **Command (⌘)** key, usually beside the space bar.
2.  To copy selected text or a file, press **Command** and **C**.
3.  To paste, press **Command** and **V**.
4.  To cut selected text, press **Command** and **X**.
5.  To undo an action, press **Command** and **Z**.
6.  To select everything in a document or folder, press **Command** and
    **A**.

These shortcuts are similar to common Windows shortcuts, but you usually
use **Command** instead of **Ctrl**. The **Option (⌥)** key is another
Mac modifier key, often used in additional shortcuts.

\[SCREENSHOT: Mac keyboard highlighting the Command and Option keys\]

> **Tip:** When you see a shortcut written with the ⌘ symbol, it means
> hold down Command while pressing the other key. You do not need to
> press the keys one after another.

## Step 3: Find your files with Finder

Finder is the place to browse, organize, and search for files on your
Mac. If you are used to File Explorer, think of Finder as its Mac
equivalent.

1.  Click the **Finder** icon in the Dock.
2.  Look in the sidebar for common locations such as **Recents**,
    **Applications**, **Desktop**, **Documents**, and **Downloads**.
3.  Click **Documents** to see files saved in that folder.
4.  Click **Downloads** to find files you have downloaded from the
    internet.
5.  Use the search field in a Finder window to look for a file by name.

You can create a folder by opening the location where you want it, then
choosing **File \> New Folder** from the menu bar. Give the folder a
name and press **Return**.

\[SCREENSHOT: Finder window with the sidebar showing Documents,
Downloads, Desktop, and Applications\]

A useful difference to remember: closing a window does not always quit
the app. The app may remain open, with its name still visible in the
menu bar. To quit the current app, choose **App Name \> Quit App Name**
from the menu bar, or press **Command** and **Q**.

## Step 4: Connect to Wi-Fi and adjust settings

On Windows, you may be used to opening Settings from the Start menu. On
a Mac, most computer-wide options are in **System Settings**.

1.  Click the **Apple menu** in the top-left corner.
2.  Choose **System Settings**.
3.  Select **Wi-Fi** in the sidebar.
4.  Turn Wi-Fi on if necessary, then choose your network.
5.  Enter the Wi-Fi password and click **Join**.

You can return to **System Settings** to adjust other things, such as
sound, display, Bluetooth, notifications, and your wallpaper. If you
cannot find a setting, use the search field in the System Settings
window and type what you want to change.

\[SCREENSHOT: System Settings showing the Wi-Fi panel and available
network list\]

> **Careful:** If you are connecting to a public Wi-Fi network, check
> that its name is correct before joining. Avoid entering sensitive
> information on a network you do not trust.

## Step 5: Install and open apps

Windows programs and Mac apps are not always interchangeable. A Windows
installer file, such as an .exe file, generally will not install
directly on macOS. You need the Mac version of the app, if one is
available.

1.  Open the **App Store** from the Dock or Applications folder to
    browse Mac apps.
2.  Search for the app you want.
3.  Open its listing and check that it is compatible with your Mac and
    macOS version.
4.  If you download an app from its developer's website, use the
    developer's official site.
5.  Open the downloaded app and follow its installation instructions.

Many familiar services, including email, office tools, and web browsers,
have Mac versions. Some can also be used in a web browser without
installing an app.

\[SCREENSHOT: Mac App Store showing an app listing and its Get button\]

> **Good to know:** Before paying for an app again, sign in with the
> account you used on Windows and check whether your existing
> subscription includes the Mac version.

## Step 6: Move your files from Windows

You do not have to move every file immediately. Start with the
documents, photos, and other items you know you need.

1.  On your Windows PC, gather important files into clearly named
    folders.
2.  Copy those folders to an external drive, or upload them to a cloud
    storage service you use.
3.  Connect the external drive to your Mac, if you are using one.
4.  Open **Finder** and select the drive in the sidebar.
5.  Drag the folders you want into **Documents**, **Pictures**, or
    another suitable location on your Mac.
6.  Open a few copied files to check that they transferred correctly.

If you prefer a guided transfer, Apple's **Migration Assistant** can
help move certain documents, user accounts, and settings from a Windows
PC to a Mac. The Windows computer and Mac need to meet Apple's
requirements, and the process may depend on the versions involved.
Follow Apple's current Migration Assistant instructions for the exact
steps.

\[SCREENSHOT: Finder window showing an external drive in the sidebar and
folders being copied\]

Keep the original files on your Windows PC until you have checked the
copies on your Mac. Some Windows-only apps or file types may need a
compatible Mac app to open.

## Step 7: Learn a few everyday Mac habits

A few small differences will help your new Mac feel more familiar.

-   **Right-click:** Click with two fingers on a trackpad, or use the
    right mouse button. You can adjust this in **System Settings \>
    Trackpad** or **Mouse**.
-   **Switch apps:** Press **Command** and **Tab** to move between open
    apps.
-   **Search:** Press **Command** and **Space** to open Spotlight, then
    type the name of an app, file, or setting.
-   **Take a screenshot:** Press **Shift**, **Command**, and **5** to
    open the screenshot and screen-recording controls.
-   **Lock your Mac:** Press **Control**, **Command**, and **Q** to lock
    the screen.

## Troubleshooting: A few common first-day questions

-   **Where is the Start menu?** There is no direct Start menu
    equivalent. Use the Dock, Finder, Spotlight, or the Applications
    folder to open apps.
-   **Why did the app stay open after I closed its window?** Closing a
    window is different from quitting an app. Choose **Quit** from the
    app menu or press **Command** and **Q**.
-   **Why will my Windows program not install?** Windows installer files
    are not usually compatible with macOS. Look for a Mac version or a
    web version from the software provider.
-   **Why can't I find a setting?** Open **System Settings** and search
    for the setting by name. Menu wording and layout can vary slightly
    between macOS versions.
-   **Can I keep using my Windows PC?** Yes. You can use both computers
    while you move files and get used to the Mac.

## Take your time getting comfortable

Switching from Windows to Mac is mostly a matter of learning a new
layout and a few different keyboard shortcuts. Start with the tasks you
do every day, and explore the rest as you need it. Your new Mac will
soon feel much more familiar.
