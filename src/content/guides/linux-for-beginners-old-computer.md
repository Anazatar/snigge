That old laptop gathering dust might not be ready for the trash. With a free operating system called **Linux**, many older computers become fast and useful again for browsing the web, email, video calls and writing documents.

## What is Linux?

Your computer needs an **operating system** to work. It's the main software that runs everything. Windows and macOS are operating systems. **Linux** is another one, and it's free.

There are many versions of Linux. For beginners, we recommend **Linux Mint**. It looks and feels a lot like Windows, so you'll find your way around quickly.

## Is my computer good enough?

Linux Mint needs:

- A **64-bit** computer (almost every computer from the last 15 years)
- At least **2 GB of memory (RAM)**, but 4 GB is better
- At least **20 GB of storage**, but 100 GB is better

> **Tip:** If your computer is very old or has little memory, choose the **Xfce Edition** of Linux Mint when downloading. It's lighter and runs faster on older machines.

## What you'll need

- The old computer
- A **USB flash drive** of 8 GB or more (it will be erased)
- Another computer with internet to prepare the USB drive
- About an hour

> **Careful:** Installing Linux will erase everything on the old computer. **Copy any photos and files you want to keep** to a USB drive or cloud storage before you start.

## Step 1: Download Linux Mint

1. On your working computer, go to **linuxmint.com**.
2. Click **Download**.
3. Choose an edition (**Cinnamon** for most people, **Xfce** for older computers).
4. Download the file. It ends in **.iso** and is about 3 GB.

> **Safety first:** Only download Linux Mint from the official website, linuxmint.com.

## Step 2: Put Linux Mint on the USB drive

You can't just copy the file. You need a free program to make the USB drive "bootable."

1. Download **balenaEtcher** from **etcher.balena.io** and install it.
2. Plug in your USB drive.
3. Open balenaEtcher.
4. Click **Flash from file** and choose the Linux Mint .iso file.
5. Click **Select target** and choose your USB drive.
6. Click **Flash!** and wait until it says it's finished.

## Step 3: Start the old computer from the USB drive

1. Plug the USB drive into the old computer.
2. Turn it on and **immediately** tap the boot menu key over and over. It's usually **F12**, **F11**, **F2**, **Esc** or **Delete**, depending on the brand. The key is often shown briefly on screen when the computer starts.
3. A menu appears. Choose your **USB drive** and press **Enter**.

> **Tip:** If Windows starts normally instead, restart and try a different key. Searching "boot menu key" plus your computer brand will tell you the right one.

## Step 4: Try it before installing

Linux Mint starts directly from the USB drive, **without changing anything on your computer**. Take your time: open the web browser, check that Wi-Fi and sound work, and see if you like it.

If you change your mind, just turn off the computer and remove the USB drive. Nothing was changed.

## Step 5: Install it

If you like what you see:

1. Double-click **Install Linux Mint** on the desktop.
2. Choose your language and keyboard.
3. When asked about installation type, choose **Erase disk and install Linux Mint**.
4. Pick your time zone, then create a username and password. **Write the password down.**
5. Wait for it to finish, then restart and remove the USB drive.

## Welcome to Linux!

Your old computer now has a modern, secure system that gets free updates. It comes with a web browser (Firefox), an office suite (LibreOffice) and everything you need for daily use.
