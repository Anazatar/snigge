Slow Wi-Fi can make everything harder, from video calls to watching TV. Most of the time, you can fix it yourself in a few minutes. Try these steps in order.

## Step 1: Restart your router

Your **router** is the box (usually with blinking lights) that your internet company gave you. Like any computer, it works better after a restart.

1. **Unplug** the router's power cable from the wall.
2. Wait **30 seconds**.
3. Plug it back in.
4. Wait **2 to 3 minutes** for all the lights to come back on.

> **Tip:** If you have two boxes (a **modem** and a separate **router**), unplug both. Plug the modem in first, wait for its lights, then plug in the router.

This simple step fixes the problem more often than anything else.

## Step 2: Test your speed

To know if your internet is really slow, run a free speed test.

1. Open your web browser on your phone or computer.
2. Go to **fast.com** or **speedtest.net**.
3. Wait for the result.

Compare the number with the speed on your internet bill. If it's **much lower** even when you're right next to the router, the problem may be with your internet company (see Step 6).

## Step 3: Move your router

Where your router sits makes a big difference. Wi-Fi signals get weaker through walls, floors and furniture.

**Best spots:**
- In the **middle** of your home
- **Up high**, like on a shelf
- Out in the **open**

**Avoid:**
- Inside a cabinet or closet
- On the floor or behind the TV
- Next to a **microwave**, cordless phone or fish tank (water blocks Wi-Fi)

## Step 4: Pick the right Wi-Fi network

Many routers create **two Wi-Fi networks** with similar names, for example "HomeWiFi" and "HomeWiFi-5G".

- **5 GHz** (often ending in "5G" or "5"): **faster**, but shorter range. Best when you're in the same room or close by.
- **2.4 GHz**: **slower**, but reaches farther. Better for distant rooms.

Try connecting to the 5 GHz network when you're near the router.

## Step 5: Check how many devices are connected

Phones, tablets, TVs, computers, smart speakers and game consoles all share the same Wi-Fi. If someone is streaming movies or downloading big updates, everything else slows down.

Try pausing big downloads, or turn off Wi-Fi on devices you're not using.

## Step 6: Call your internet company

If nothing helps, call your provider. Ask them to:

- **Check for problems** in your area
- **Test your line** from their end
- See if your **router is outdated** (many will replace it for free)

> **Good to know:** If your home is large or has thick walls, a **mesh Wi-Fi system** (several small units placed around the house) can bring strong Wi-Fi to every room.

## Enjoy faster Wi-Fi!

In most homes, restarting the router and moving it to a better spot is all it takes.
