Computers can break, get lost or be stolen. A backup means your photos and files are safe no matter what happens. Every Mac comes with a free backup tool called **Time Machine**. You set it up once, and it does the rest automatically.

## What you'll need

- Your Mac
- An **external hard drive** (a small box that plugs into your Mac with a cable)

> **Tip:** Choose a drive with at least **twice** the storage of your Mac. A 1 TB or 2 TB drive works well for most people and is not expensive.

> **Careful:** Time Machine will **erase** the drive the first time you use it. Use a new, empty drive, or move anything important off it first.

## Step 1: Plug in the drive

Connect the external drive to your Mac. Your Mac may ask if you want to use it for Time Machine. If it does, click **Use as Backup Disk** and skip to Step 4.

## Step 2: Open Time Machine settings

1. Click the **Apple menu** (the Apple logo) in the top-left corner of the screen.
2. Click **System Settings**.
3. Click **General** in the sidebar, then click **Time Machine**.

> **On an older Mac?** If you see **System Preferences** instead of System Settings, click it, then click **Time Machine**.

## Step 3: Choose your backup disk

1. Click **Add Backup Disk** (or the **+** button).
2. Select your external drive from the list.
3. Click **Set Up Disk**.

## Step 4: Encrypt your backup (recommended)

Your Mac will offer to **encrypt** the backup. This means nobody can read your files without a password, even if the drive is lost.

1. Turn on **Encrypt Backup**.
2. Create a password and write it down somewhere safe. **If you forget it, you can't get your files back.**
3. Click **Done**.

## Step 5: Let the first backup finish

The first backup copies everything, so it can take a few hours. You can keep using your Mac while it works. After that, backups are quick.

## How Time Machine works

As long as the drive is plugged in, Time Machine automatically saves:

- **Hourly** backups for the past 24 hours
- **Daily** backups for the past month
- **Weekly** backups for all previous months

When the drive gets full, the oldest backups are deleted to make room.

> **Tip:** If you use a laptop and don't keep the drive plugged in, connect it at least once a week so Time Machine can catch up.

## How to get a file back

Deleted something by mistake? Here's how to bring it back.

1. Open the folder where the file used to be (for example, **Documents**).
2. Click the **Time Machine** icon in the menu bar at the top of the screen (it looks like a clock with an arrow) and choose **Browse Time Machine Backups**.
3. Use the arrows or timeline on the right to go back in time.
4. Select the file and click **Restore**.

## You're protected!

That's all there is to it. Your Mac is now backing itself up, and your memories are safe.
