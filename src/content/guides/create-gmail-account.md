Having an email address makes it easier to keep in touch with family, receive bills and appointment reminders, and sign up for online services. If you do not have one yet, creating a Gmail account is a straightforward place to start.

Gmail is Google's free email service. When you create a Gmail account, you also create a Google Account, which you can use with services such as Google Drive, Google Calendar, and YouTube. This guide walks you through setting it up on a computer or phone.

## What you'll need

- A computer, smartphone, or tablet with an internet connection.
- A mobile phone number, if Google asks you to verify your sign-up.
- An existing email address for recovery, if you have one. This is optional during some sign-up flows but useful for account recovery.

## Step 1: Open Google's account creation page

Start from Google's official sign-up page. This helps you avoid lookalike websites that may try to collect your personal details.

1. Open a web browser, such as Chrome, Safari, Edge, or Firefox.
2. Type **accounts.google.com/signup** into the address bar and press **Enter**.
3. Check that the page belongs to Google and says **Create a Google Account**.
4. Choose **For my personal use** if Google asks what the account is for.

[SCREENSHOT: Google's Create a Google Account page in a web browser]

You can also begin from the Gmail app. Open the app, choose **Add another account** or **Add account**, select **Google**, and follow the sign-up prompts. The wording can vary slightly between Android and iPhone.

> **Careful:** Make sure you are on Google's genuine website before entering personal information. Do not create an account through a link in an unexpected email or text message.

## Step 2: Enter your name and basic details

Google asks for some information to create the account. The exact screens may appear in a slightly different order depending on your device and Google's current sign-up process.

1. Enter your first name and last name in the boxes provided.
2. Select your date of birth and any other requested details.
3. Follow the prompts and select **Next** when ready.
4. If Google asks for a gender selection, choose an available option or use the option to decline if shown.

Use accurate information that you can remember. Google may use some of these details to help verify your identity or recover access to the account.

[SCREENSHOT: Google sign-up form showing the name and basic information fields, with example details]

> **Good to know:** Google's sign-up screens can change. If a field or option looks different from this guide, follow the instructions shown on the official Google page.

## Step 3: Choose your Gmail address

Your Gmail address is the name people will use to send you email. It usually ends in **@gmail.com**.

1. When asked to choose your Gmail address, type the name you would like to use.
2. Try a simple version of your name that is easy to tell someone over the phone.
3. If Google says the address is unavailable, try another variation.
4. Select one of Google's suggested addresses if you prefer.
5. Click **Next** to continue.

Your address needs to be unique. If someone else already has the one you want, you cannot use exactly the same address. You might try adding a middle initial or a few numbers that are easy for you to remember.

[SCREENSHOT: Gmail address selection screen showing an example address and an availability message]

> **Tip:** Choose an address you will feel comfortable sharing with family, friends, and organizations. Avoid including sensitive details such as your full date of birth or home address.

## Step 4: Create a strong password

Your password helps protect your Gmail and the other Google services connected to the account. Choose one that is difficult for someone else to guess and that you do not use for another website.

1. Enter your chosen password in the **Password** box.
2. Type it again in the confirmation box if asked.
3. Check that both entries match.
4. Select **Next**.

A long passphrase made from several unrelated words can be easier to remember than a short password with complicated characters. You can also use a reputable password manager to create and store a unique password.

[SCREENSHOT: Google password creation screen with the password hidden]

Do not share your password with anyone who contacts you unexpectedly. Google will not need you to tell a caller or message sender your password to complete account setup.

## Step 5: Add recovery information and complete verification

Google may ask for a phone number or another email address. These details can help you regain access if you forget your password or Google needs to check that it is really you.

1. If asked for a phone number, enter a number you can access and follow the verification instructions.
2. Google may send a text message with a code. Enter that code on the official sign-up page when requested.
3. If asked for a recovery email, enter an email address you can access. Check it carefully for typing mistakes.
4. Review any additional privacy or account options and follow the prompts.

Google does not ask every person for exactly the same verification steps. A phone number may be required in some circumstances, while in other sign-ups you may be able to skip it. Follow the options Google presents rather than relying on a fixed sequence.

[SCREENSHOT: Google account verification screen with a code entry field; use a fictional example and no real phone number]

> **Good to know:** A recovery email or phone number is different from your Gmail address. It gives Google another way to help confirm your identity if you are locked out. Keep recovery details up to date.

## Step 6: Review the terms and finish creating your account

Before the account is ready, Google asks you to review its terms and privacy information.

1. Read the information on the screen. You can use the links to learn how Google handles account information.
2. Scroll through the page as needed.
3. Select **I agree** or the confirmation button shown to finish.
4. Wait for Google to complete the setup.
5. If prompted, open Gmail to see your new inbox.

[SCREENSHOT: Google terms and privacy confirmation screen with the final agreement button visible]

Your new Gmail inbox may contain a welcome message from Google. You can now send and receive email using your new address.

## Step 7: Send yourself a test email

A quick test helps you confirm that your new address is working.

1. Open Gmail at **gmail.com**, or open the Gmail app.
2. Sign in with your new Gmail address and password if asked.
3. Select **Compose**.
4. In the **To** box, enter your new Gmail address.
5. Add a short subject, such as “Test email”, and type a brief message.
6. Select **Send**.
7. Open your **Inbox** and check that the message arrives.

[SCREENSHOT: Gmail Compose window with a sample test email addressed to the same account]

## Troubleshooting: If you cannot create your account

**The Gmail address is already taken.** Try another variation. Google will not let two people use the same Gmail address.

**Google asks for phone verification.** Follow the instructions on the official page and use a number you can access. If a code does not arrive, check the number and country code, then follow Google's on-screen options.

**I did not receive a verification code.** Check that your phone has signal and that the number was entered correctly. Wait briefly before requesting another code, and do not share the code with anyone.

**I cannot remember my password.** If you have completed setup, use Google's official account recovery page at **accounts.google.com/signin/recovery**. Follow the prompts from a device and location you normally use, if possible.

**I already have a Google Account.** You may not need to create another one. You can use your existing Google Account to sign in to Gmail, or add a Gmail address if your account does not have one.

## Your Gmail account is ready

Creating a Gmail account takes only a few steps: choose an address, set a strong password, and add recovery information. Keep your sign-in details private, make sure your recovery options stay current, and you will be ready to use Gmail for everyday messages.
