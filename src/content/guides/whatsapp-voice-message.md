WhatsApp voice messages let you speak instead of typing. They are useful
when you have a lot to say, when typing is awkward, or when you want
someone to hear the tone of your voice.

You can send a voice message in a private conversation or a group chat.
The steps are slightly different depending on whether you use an iPhone,
Android phone, or computer, but the microphone control is easy to spot.

## 1. Open the WhatsApp conversation

Start by opening WhatsApp on your phone. Find the person or group you
want to contact, then tap the conversation to open it.

If you have not chatted with that person before, use the new-chat button
to find and select their name. Check the name at the top of the
conversation before recording, especially if you are sending a personal
message.

> **Tip:** A voice message goes to the chat that is open. Take a moment
> to check that you have selected the right person or group.

## 2. Find the microphone button

Look beside the message box at the bottom of the screen. You should see
a microphone icon near the place where you normally type.

If you see a camera or another control instead, tap the microphone or
switch the recording control back to voice mode. The exact appearance
can vary slightly between app versions.

On WhatsApp for a computer, open the conversation and look for the
microphone icon beside the message field. Your computer may ask for
permission to use its microphone the first time.

> **Screenshot suggestion:** Show an open WhatsApp chat with the message
> field and microphone icon clearly visible. Do not include real contact
> names or private messages.

## 3. Record and send a voice message on iPhone or Android

The simplest way to record is to press and hold the microphone button
while you speak.

1.  Open the chat where you want to send the message.
2.  Press and hold the **Microphone** button.
3.  Keep holding it while you speak clearly.
4.  When you finish, lift your finger. WhatsApp sends the recording
    automatically.

Try speaking at a comfortable pace and keep the phone close enough to
pick up your voice. You do not need to shout.

If you change your mind while holding the button, slide your finger to
the left to cancel the recording. Do not lift your finger if you intend
to cancel; lifting it normally sends the message.

## 4. Record hands-free with the lock control

You do not have to keep your finger on the microphone for a longer
message. While recording, swipe upward from the microphone button to
lock the recording.

Once the recording is locked, you can take your finger off the screen
and continue speaking. When you finish, use the on-screen controls to
stop, review, or send the recording.

This is handy if you want to hold the phone more comfortably or need
both hands free. Take care not to tap **Send** until you are ready for
the message to go out.

> **Remember:** Releasing the microphone during an ordinary
> press-and-hold recording sends the message. Swipe up to lock it before
> taking your finger away.

## 5. Preview, pause, or delete a recording before sending

When you use the locked recording controls, WhatsApp may let you pause
and listen to a draft before sending it. This gives you a chance to
check that the message is clear.

1.  Tap the **Pause** control to stop recording temporarily.
2.  Tap **Play** to listen to the draft.
3.  If you want to add more, tap the microphone or resume control to
    continue recording.
4.  If you do not want to send it, use the delete or discard control.
5.  When you are happy with the recording, tap **Send**.

The controls can look a little different across devices and app
versions. If you do not see a preview option, you can still make a short
test recording in a chat with someone you trust.

## 6. Send a voice message from WhatsApp on a computer

You can also record voice messages in WhatsApp Web or the WhatsApp
desktop app, if your browser or computer supports microphone access.

1.  Open WhatsApp Web or the desktop app and select a conversation.
2.  Click the **Microphone** icon beside the message field.
3.  If prompted, allow WhatsApp or your browser to use the microphone.
4.  Speak into your computer's built-in microphone or a connected
    headset.
5.  Use the on-screen control to finish and send the recording. If a
    review or cancel option appears, choose the one you want before
    sending.

If the microphone icon is missing or unavailable, update WhatsApp or try
the desktop app or supported browser again. Computer permissions can
also prevent recording.

## 7. Check whether someone has played your voice message

After you send a voice message, it appears in the conversation as an
audio bubble with a waveform. The waveform is a visual pattern that
represents the sound.

WhatsApp uses microphone indicators to show playback status. A gray
microphone generally means the message has not been played by everyone
in the conversation, although some recipients may have listened. A blue
microphone indicates that all recipients have played it.

In a group chat, remember that different people may listen at different
times. The indicator is not a guarantee that everyone has understood or
acted on what you said.

## Troubleshooting: When voice messages do not work

**The microphone button is missing.** Make sure the message field is
visible and that voice recording is selected rather than video-message
mode. Update WhatsApp if the control still does not appear.

**WhatsApp cannot access the microphone.** Open your phone's Settings
app, find WhatsApp in the app permissions, and allow microphone access.
On a computer, check the browser or operating-system microphone
permissions. Menu names vary by device.

**The recording is silent or hard to hear.** Check that the microphone
opening is not covered by a case or your hand. Disconnect a headset or
Bluetooth device if sound is being captured by the wrong microphone,
then try again in a quiet place.

**The recording stops or disappears.** Check your internet connection
and make sure WhatsApp is up to date. If a recording disappears before
you can send it, close and reopen WhatsApp and try again.

**The message went before you were ready.** During a press-and-hold
recording, lifting your finger normally sends the message. For a longer
recording, swipe up to lock it, then use the preview and send controls.

> **Privacy note:** WhatsApp says personal voice messages are protected
> with end-to-end encryption. This means the message is designed to be
> readable or playable only by the people in the conversation, not by
> someone intercepting it in transit. Still, only send recordings you
> are comfortable sharing with the recipients.

## A simple way to keep in touch

Sending a voice message on WhatsApp is as simple as opening a chat,
holding the microphone, and speaking. For longer recordings, lock the
microphone so you can pause and review the message before sending it.

Try a short message first to get used to the controls. Once you know
where the microphone and cancel buttons are, you can use voice messages
whenever speaking feels easier than typing.
