If you want to **back up your Android phone**, you may be wondering how to keep your contacts, messages, and settings safe if your phone is lost, damaged, or replaced. Google has a built-in backup service that can save much of this information to the Google Account linked to your phone.

Once you switch it on, your phone can back up automatically. You can also start a backup yourself and check when it last finished. The exact menu names vary between Android phones, so this guide includes the common route and a separate note for Samsung Galaxy phones.

## What you'll need

- Your Android phone and its screen lock PIN, pattern, or password.
- The Google Account you want to use for the backup.
- A Wi-Fi connection.
- Enough available Google Account storage.

> **Good to know:** Google Account storage is shared across services such as Google Drive, Gmail, and Google Photos. A full account may not have enough room for a new backup.

## Step 1: Check that your Google Account is on your phone

Your backup is saved to a Google Account, so first make sure the account you want to use is signed in.

1. Open your phone's **Settings** app.
2. Look for **Passwords & accounts**, **Accounts**, or **Google**, depending on your phone.
3. Check that the Google Account you want to use appears in the list.
4. If you need to add an account, choose **Add account** > **Google** and follow the sign-in instructions.

[SCREENSHOT: Android Settings showing the Google Account listed under Accounts]

If you have more than one Google Account, take a moment to check which one you want to use. Your backup will be associated with the selected account, so make sure you can sign in to it later.

> **Careful:** Make sure you know the password for the Google Account you choose and that its recovery details are up to date. You may need access to this account when setting up a replacement phone.

## Step 2: Find the Google backup settings

On many Android phones, the backup controls are inside the Google section of Settings.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Google**.
3. Look for **All services** if it appears, then find **Backup** under **Backup and restore**.
4. Tap **Backup** to open the backup settings.

Depending on your Android version and phone maker, you may instead find **Backup** under **System**. If the menu names do not match, use the search box at the top of **Settings** and search for “Backup.”

[SCREENSHOT: Android Settings showing Google services and the Backup option]

**If you have a Samsung Galaxy phone:** Open **Settings** > **Accounts and backup**. Under **Google Drive**, tap **Back up data**. Make sure you choose the Google Drive option if your aim is to back up to your Google Account; Samsung Cloud is a separate service.

## Step 3: Turn on Google backup

Once you have found the backup page, switch on the backup service and review the information it can save.

1. On the **Backup** screen, follow the prompt to turn on backup if it is not already enabled.
2. If you see a switch called **Backup by Google One** or **Backup**, turn it on.
3. If your phone offers a choice of backup account, select the Google Account you checked in Step 1.
4. Review the categories or backup details shown on the screen.

Some phones show separate choices for **Device data** and **Photos & videos**. The available choices depend on your phone and software version.

[SCREENSHOT: Android Google Backup screen showing backup enabled and backup details]

> **Good to know:** A Google device backup can include apps and some app data, call history, contacts, device settings, and SMS and MMS messages. Not every app saves all its information, so some app data may not transfer to a new phone.

## Step 4: Back up your photos and videos separately

One important detail: the standard Android device backup does not necessarily save your entire photo library. Photos and videos are usually backed up separately through Google Photos.

1. Open the **Google Photos** app.
2. Tap your profile picture or initial in the top corner.
3. Tap **Photos settings** > **Backup**. The wording may vary slightly by app version.
4. Turn on **Backup** if you want your photos and videos saved to your Google Photos library.
5. Check which Google Account is shown at the top of the backup settings.

[SCREENSHOT: Google Photos Backup settings showing the Backup switch and account]

Google Photos may take time to upload a large collection, particularly if your internet connection is slow. Keep the phone connected to Wi-Fi and power while the first upload completes.

> **Tip:** Check Google Photos from another device or at photos.google.com to confirm that recent pictures have appeared. This is a useful separate check from the phone's device backup.

## Step 5: Start a backup now

After enabling automatic backup, you can ask your phone to make a backup straight away.

1. Return to **Settings** > **Google** > **All services** > **Backup**. On some phones, open **Settings** > **System** > **Backup** instead.
2. Check that the correct Google Account is selected.
3. Tap **Back up now** if the option is available.
4. Keep your phone connected to Wi-Fi while it works.

On a Samsung Galaxy, go to **Settings** > **Accounts and backup** > **Back up data** under **Google Drive**, then tap **Back up now** if shown.

[SCREENSHOT: Android Backup screen with the Back up now option]

The first backup can take a while. Google says a device backup may take up to 24 hours to complete, so don't worry if it does not finish immediately.

## Step 6: Check that the backup has finished

Before relying on the backup, check its status and the date of the latest successful backup.

1. Open the same **Backup** screen in **Settings**.
2. Look for the last backup time or status.
3. Review **Backup details** to see which types of information are included.
4. If you enabled Google Photos backup, open Google Photos and check its backup status separately.

[SCREENSHOT: Android Backup details showing the latest backup status and included data]

If the backup is still pending, leave the phone connected to Wi-Fi and power and check again later. A message showing that backup is on does not always mean the first upload has finished.

## Troubleshooting: If your Android phone won't back up

If backup is not starting or keeps stopping, try these checks:

- **Not enough storage:** Check your available Google Account storage. Google Accounts include 15 GB of storage at no charge, shared across Google Drive, Gmail, and Google Photos. You may need to make room or choose a larger storage plan.
- **No Wi-Fi connection:** Connect to a reliable Wi-Fi network and try again. Some backup activity may be limited when you use mobile data.
- **Wrong Google Account:** Return to the backup settings and check the account shown. Switch to the account you intend to use if necessary.
- **Backup is taking a long time:** Large backups can take many hours. Keep the phone connected to power and Wi-Fi and check the status later.
- **You cannot find the menu:** Search for “Backup” in the Settings app. On Samsung Galaxy phones, look under **Accounts and backup**; on other Android phones, check **Google** or **System**.
- **Some information is missing:** Not all apps support Google backup. Check whether the app has its own backup or account-sync setting.

## Your Android phone is better protected

With Google backup switched on, your phone can regularly save supported information to your Google Account. Check the backup date occasionally, and remember to check Google Photos separately if you want your pictures and videos protected too.
