Your phone rings and the caller says they are from your bank. They may
claim there is a suspicious payment, a problem with your card, or an
urgent security issue. The call can sound convincing, especially if the
caller already knows your name or a few details about you.

A **fake bank call scam** is when someone pretends to work for your bank
to trick you into sharing information, moving money, or approving a
payment. The safest response is simple: do not rely on the caller's
identity or the number displayed on your phone. End the call and contact
your bank using a number or app you already trust.

## Step 1: Recognize the warning signs of a fake bank call

Scammers often try to create urgency. They want you to feel that you
must act immediately, before you have time to check what is happening.

Be cautious if a caller:

1.  Says there is a suspicious payment and you must move your money to a
    "safe" account.
2.  Asks for your full password, PIN, card security code, or one-time
    sign-in code.
3.  Tells you to approve a notification or payment in your banking app
    that you did not start.
4.  Asks you to install an app or give them remote access to your phone
    or computer.
5.  Pressures you to keep the call secret, even from bank staff, family,
    or the police.
6.  Offers to send a courier to collect your bank card or asks you to
    hand it to someone.
7.  Demands payment using gift cards, cryptocurrency, or a transfer to a
    personal account.

A genuine bank may contact you about account activity, but you should
never have to disclose your full PIN or password to prove who you are.
If something feels unusual, stop and check independently.

## Step 2: Do not trust the caller ID or the details they know

Your phone may display your bank's name or a familiar telephone number.
That does not prove the call is genuine.

Scammers can use a technique called **caller ID spoofing**. This means
they make a call appear to come from a different phone number, sometimes
one that belongs to a real bank.

They may also know your name, address, or the last few digits of a card.
Information like this can come from data breaches, public sources, or
previous scams. It does not prove the caller works for your bank.

1.  Treat an unexpected request for money or security details with
    caution.
2.  Do not use a phone number supplied by the caller.
3.  Do not assume a familiar voice or professional manner proves the
    person's identity.
4.  Remember that a caller can sound calm and helpful while still trying
    to trick you.

> **Good to know:** Caller ID is useful for recognizing familiar
> contacts, but it is not a reliable way to verify a bank. Always check

## Step 3: End the call and contact your bank safely

If you are unsure whether a call is genuine, you do not need to argue or
answer more questions. End the call and make a fresh contact with your
bank.

1.  Hang up. Do not press buttons or follow instructions given by the
    caller.
2.  Find your bank's number on the back of your bank card, on an
    official statement, or inside its official app.
3.  If you use online banking, open the app yourself rather than
    following a link sent during the call.
4.  Tell the bank you received a suspicious call and ask whether there
    is a genuine issue with your account.
5.  Follow the bank's advice before taking any action on a payment or
    account alert.

If you are using the same landline that received the call, wait a few
minutes before calling, or use a different phone. Some phone scams try
to keep a line open after the victim thinks the call has ended.

## Step 4: Protect your passwords and security codes

Your bank may use security checks to confirm your identity when you
contact it. However, an unexpected caller should not ask you to reveal
your full password, PIN, or a one-time code that lets them sign in or
approve an action.

A **one-time passcode** is a code that is valid for a short time, often
sent by text or generated in an app. Scammers may try to persuade you to
read it aloud, claiming it is needed to cancel a payment or block a
fraudster.

1.  Never read a sign-in or payment approval code to an unexpected
    caller.
2.  Do not approve a banking app notification unless you started the
    action and understand what it is for.
3.  Do not share your full online banking password or card PIN.
4.  If you have shared a code or password, contact your bank immediately
    using a trusted number.
5.  Change affected passwords through the official app or website if
    your bank advises you to do so.

## Step 5: Know what to do if you have already shared information

If you have shared a code, password, card details, or sent money, act
quickly. You do not need to feel embarrassed or wait until you know
exactly what happened.

1.  Contact your bank immediately using the number on your card or its
    official app.
2.  Explain what the caller asked you to do and what information or
    money you shared.
3.  Ask the bank to secure your account, check recent activity, and
    attempt to stop or recall any suspicious payment where possible.
4.  Follow its instructions about cards, passwords, and online banking
    access.
5.  Keep call records, messages, emails, and payment details in case
    they are needed for a report.

If you installed an app or gave the caller access to your device, tell
the bank. Use a different device you trust to contact the bank if
possible, and ask for advice about checking the affected phone or
computer.

## Step 6: Report fake bank calls in the US or UK

Reporting suspicious calls can help authorities and banks identify scam
patterns. You can report an attempted scam even if you did not lose
money.

-   **United States:** Report the incident to the Federal Trade
    Commission at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).
    If money or bank information was involved, contact your bank first.
-   **United Kingdom:** Report suspicious calls and fraud through
    [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/). Report Fraud
    replaced Action Fraud in December 2025. You can also report
    suspicious text messages by forwarding them to **7726** (the word
    "SPAM" on most UK networks).
-   **Scotland:** You can contact Police Scotland on **101** to report
    fraud or cybercrime.

Your bank may also have a dedicated fraud reporting process in its app
or on its official website. Use contact details you find independently,
not information provided by the caller.

## Troubleshooting: Common questions about bank calls

**Can my bank call me about suspicious activity?**

Yes, banks may contact customers about account activity or security
checks. But you can always end an unexpected call and contact the bank
yourself through its official app or a verified number.

**What if the caller knows my name and address?**

That does not prove they are genuine. Personal details can be obtained
from many sources. Do not share passwords, codes, or payment information
just because the caller knows something about you.

**What if the number on my screen matches my bank?**

The number may have been spoofed. Do not use caller ID as proof. End the
call and use the number on your card or in your official banking app.

**Should I press a button to cancel a suspicious payment?**

Do not follow instructions from an unexpected caller. Open your bank's
app yourself or contact the bank through a verified number and ask it to
check the payment.

## Stay in control of the call

A real bank will understand if you want to verify a call before
discussing your account. Never share your PIN, password, or security
codes with an unexpected caller, and never move money because someone
pressures you to do so. Hang up, contact your bank independently, and
take your time.
