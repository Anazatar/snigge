Meeting people online can be a positive way to make connections, whether through a dating site, social media, or a messaging app. But some people use these spaces to deceive others and take advantage of their trust.

A romance scam happens when someone pretends to have romantic feelings in order to get money, gifts, or personal information. Knowing the common romance scam signs can help you take things slowly, check what you are being told, and protect yourself without feeling embarrassed or blamed.

## What you'll need

- Access to the dating site, social media account, or messaging service where you met the person.
- A trusted friend or family member you can talk things through with.
- The official contact details for your bank if you are worried that you have sent money or shared financial information.

## Step 1: Notice when the relationship moves unusually fast

A scammer may try to create a strong emotional connection very quickly. They might say they have never felt this way before, talk about a future together after only a short time, or ask you to trust them before you know much about them.

1. Notice how quickly the person is making promises or talking about a serious future.
2. Be cautious if they use intense affection before you have had time to get to know each other.
3. Watch for claims that you are the only person who understands them or that your relationship is somehow different from every other relationship.
4. Give yourself time to decide what feels comfortable.
5. Do not let someone else's urgency set the pace of your decisions.

A fast-moving relationship is not automatically a scam. The concern is a pattern of pressure, secrecy, inconsistent stories, or requests that benefit the other person.

> **Tip:** You do not owe anyone quick commitment, private information, or money. A respectful person will accept your pace and boundaries.

## Step 2: Be cautious if they avoid meeting or having a live conversation

Someone who claims to want a relationship but always has a reason they cannot meet or speak live may be hiding something. Scammers sometimes claim to be working overseas, serving in the military, working on an oil rig, or traveling for an important job.

1. Notice whether they repeatedly cancel planned video calls or meetings.
2. Be cautious if their camera never works or they always have an excuse not to speak live.
3. Watch for stories that make an in-person meeting impossible for months or years.
4. Do not send money to help them travel, leave a job, or overcome a supposed emergency.
5. Keep your own plans and decisions separate from promises about a future meeting.

There can be genuine reasons why someone cannot meet immediately, especially if they live far away. What matters is whether their explanations remain consistent and whether they respect your questions.

## Step 3: Look for inconsistent details and suspicious profiles

A scammer may use stolen photos or invent a background that is difficult to verify. Their profile might look polished, but details in their messages may not match what they said earlier.

1. Pay attention to changes in their name, age, location, job, or personal history.
2. Be wary if their photos and stories seem unusually glamorous or too good to be true.
3. Notice whether they avoid ordinary questions about their life.
4. If you feel unsure, you can use a reputable reverse image search to see whether a profile photo appears elsewhere online.
5. Remember that a matching photo does not prove the person is genuine, and a lack of search results does not prove that they are real.

A reverse image search lets you look for other places where the same or a similar image appears. It can provide a clue, but it cannot confirm someone's identity.

> **Good to know:** Scammers sometimes use photos of real people without permission. Do not contact or accuse the person in the photo; the person pictured may also be a victim.

## Step 4: Treat requests for money as a serious warning sign

One of the clearest romance scam signs is a request for money. The person may describe an emergency, a medical bill, a travel problem, a lost wallet, a frozen bank account, or a business opportunity.

1. Be alert if the person asks you to send money, buy gift cards, or pay a bill for them.
2. Be especially cautious if they ask for cryptocurrency, wire transfers, or money through a payment app.
3. Do not share your banking details or let them use your account to receive or transfer money.
4. Do not accept a check or payment on their behalf and send part of the money elsewhere.
5. Pause all payments and speak to someone you trust before making any financial decision.

A person may have a genuine problem, but you cannot safely verify an online story simply because it sounds convincing. A genuine relationship should not depend on you sending money.

> **Careful:** Never send money or gift cards to someone you have only met online. Payments sent this way can be very difficult to recover.

## Step 5: Protect your personal information and accounts

A romance scammer may want more than money. They may ask for details that could help them access your accounts, impersonate you, or pressure you later.

1. Do not share passwords, one-time security codes, or answers to account security questions.
2. Avoid sending copies of identity documents, bank statements, or private financial information.
3. Be careful with intimate or highly personal images. Once sent, you may lose control over where they are shared.
4. Do not install an app or give someone remote access to your phone or computer because they ask you to.
5. Use unique passwords and turn on two-step verification for important accounts.

Two-step verification adds another check when someone tries to sign in, such as a code or approval on your device. It can make an account harder to access with a stolen password alone.

## Step 6: Talk to someone and verify concerns independently

Scammers often try to make a person feel that nobody else would understand the relationship. They may ask you to keep it secret or say that friends and family are jealous.

1. Talk with a trusted friend or relative about what has been happening.
2. Share the person's requests and explanations, not just the romantic messages.
3. Listen if someone points out inconsistencies you may have missed.
4. Check claims about a company, charity, travel booking, or emergency through official contact details you find independently.
5. Do not use phone numbers, links, or payment instructions supplied only by the person you are checking.

A second opinion can help you see a situation more clearly. You do not have to prove that someone is a scammer before deciding to stop communicating or refuse a request.

## Troubleshooting: What if you think you are being scammed?

If the person asks for money, pressures you, or gives explanations that do not add up, stop and protect yourself.

**Stop sending money.** Do not make another payment, even if the person promises to repay you or says one final payment will solve everything.

**Contact your bank or payment provider.** If you have sent money or shared financial details, contact them promptly using the number on your card or their official website. Ask whether they can stop or recall the payment and secure your account.

**Save relevant records.** Keep messages, usernames, email addresses, payment receipts, and transaction references. Do not alter the records. They may help a platform, bank, or law enforcement agency understand what happened.

**Report the account.** Use the dating service or social media platform's report function. You can then block the account so the person cannot continue contacting you through that profile.

**Secure your accounts.** If you shared a password or security code, change affected passwords from a trusted device, sign out of unfamiliar sessions, and enable two-step verification. If you installed software at their request, seek trusted technical help.

In the US, you can report a romance scam to the Federal Trade Commission at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) and find guidance from the [FBI](https://www.ic3.gov/). In the UK, report fraud through [Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/). If you are elsewhere, contact your country's official fraud-reporting service or local police.

If you have been affected, remember that it is not your fault. These scams are designed to manipulate trust and emotion, and anyone can be targeted.

## Take your time and trust your boundaries

The most important romance scam signs include intense pressure, repeated excuses to avoid meeting, inconsistent stories, and requests for money or private information. Take your time, talk to someone you trust, and verify important claims independently. You can end contact whenever a situation does not feel right.
