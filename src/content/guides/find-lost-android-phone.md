Misplacing your phone is unsettling, especially when it holds your photos, messages, and personal information. If you are trying to find my lost Android phone, Google's built-in Find Hub service is a good place to start.

Find Hub can show your phone's location on a map, make it ring, or help you lock it remotely. You can use it from another Android device or a web browser. The service was previously called Find My Device, so you may still see that older name on some phones or help pages.

## What you'll need

- Access to a web browser or another Android phone or tablet.
- The Google Account used on the missing phone.
- Your Google Account password and, if requested, the phone's screen lock PIN.

## Step 1: Open Google's Find Hub

The quickest way to look for a missing Android phone is to use Google's Find Hub website. You do not need to have the missing phone in your hands.

1. On another phone, tablet, or computer, open a web browser.
2. Go to [android.com/find](https://android.com/find).
3. Sign in with the Google Account used on your missing Android phone.
4. If more than one device appears, select the phone you are looking for.
5. If asked, follow the identity check. Google may ask for the screen lock PIN of the missing device or your Google Account password.

You can also use the **Find Hub** app on another Android phone. If you are borrowing someone else's phone, choose the guest sign-in option and let the phone's owner sign in to their own Google Account. Do not leave your account signed in on someone else's device.

> **Tip:** If you cannot remember which Google Account is on the missing phone, try the account you use for Google Play, Gmail, or Google Photos.

## Step 2: Check the phone's location on the map

After selecting your phone, Find Hub should show its location or its last known location, if one is available.

1. Look at the map and the location information beside your phone.
2. Check when the location was last updated, if that information is shown.
3. If the phone appears nearby, use the map to work out which room or area to check.
4. If you need help getting to the location, choose **Get directions** if it is available.

A map location is an estimate, not a guarantee that the phone is exactly at the pin. Indoor locations, underground areas, and buildings can make location information less precise.

If the phone is switched off, has no internet connection, or cannot be reached, Find Hub may show an earlier location instead. Some supported phones can still be located through the Find Hub network while offline, depending on their settings and conditions.

[SCREENSHOT: Find Hub map displaying the selected phone's location and last-seen information]

> **Good to know:** If the map shows a place you do not recognize, do not assume the phone is definitely there. Check the location time and accuracy before deciding what to do.

## Step 3: Make your phone ring

If the map suggests your phone is somewhere nearby, use the sound feature. This is useful when the phone has slipped behind a cushion or been left in another room.

1. In Find Hub, select your missing phone.
2. Choose **Play sound**.
3. Listen for the phone's ringtone.
4. Follow the sound to find it.

The phone rings at full volume for five minutes, even if it was set to silent or vibrate. When you find it, unlock it or use the on-screen controls to stop the sound.

[SCREENSHOT: Find Hub device controls with Play sound selected]

## Step 4: Lock the phone if you cannot find it

If the phone is not nearby, or you are worried someone else may have it, mark it as lost. This locks the device and lets you display a message or contact number on its lock screen.

1. Select your missing phone in Find Hub.
2. Choose **Mark as lost**.
3. Follow the on-screen instructions.
4. Add a short message and a phone number or email address where someone can reach you.
5. Confirm by selecting **Mark as lost**.

Use contact details that are safe to display publicly. For example, you might provide an alternate phone number or an email address you can access from another device. Do not put your home address or other private information in the message.

[SCREENSHOT: Mark as lost screen with fields for a lock-screen message and contact details]

> **Careful:** A lost-phone message can be seen by anyone who finds the device. Share only contact information you are comfortable making visible.

## Step 5: Protect your accounts and mobile service

If you think the phone has been stolen, or it contains information you are concerned about, take a few additional steps from a safe device.

1. Change your Google Account password if you believe someone may know it.
2. Review the devices signed in to your Google Account and sign out of any device you do not recognize.
3. Contact your mobile network provider and ask about suspending the SIM or service.
4. If the phone may have been stolen, report it to the police and provide the device details they request.
5. If you use banking or payment apps, contact the relevant provider if you are concerned that someone could access them.

Do not go to a location shown on the map to confront someone or try to recover a phone yourself. If you believe it has been stolen, let the police handle recovery.

## Step 6: Erase the phone only if necessary

If you do not expect to recover the phone and are concerned about the information on it, Find Hub may offer a remote erase or factory reset option. This is a last resort because it permanently deletes data stored on the phone.

1. Select the missing phone in Find Hub.
2. Open the device settings or available security options.
3. Choose **Factory reset** or the erase option shown.
4. Read the warning carefully.
5. Confirm only if you are sure you want to erase the device.

After the phone is erased, you will no longer be able to locate it through Find Hub. Data on a removable SD card may not be erased. If you later recover the phone, you will need the Google Account password previously used on it to set it up again.

## Troubleshooting: If Find Hub cannot find your phone

If your phone does not appear or its location is unavailable, try these checks:

- **Check the Google Account.** Make sure you signed in with the account used on the missing phone.
- **Look for a last known location.** The current location may be unavailable, but an earlier location could still help.
- **Allow time for updates.** If the phone is offline, the map may not refresh until it reconnects or is detected by the Find Hub network.
- **Check the device list.** If you have more than one Android phone or tablet, make sure you selected the correct one.
- **Use the official help page.** Google's [Find, secure, or erase a lost Android device guide](https://support.google.com/android/answer/6160491) explains the available options and requirements.

Find Hub works best when the phone has a Google Account, a screen lock, and the relevant finding settings enabled. On many Android phones, Find Hub is switched on automatically when a Google Account is added, but availability can depend on the phone's settings, power, and connection.

## A few steps can help protect your missing phone

Start by checking Find Hub and playing a sound if the phone is close by. If it is not, mark it as lost and protect your accounts. Take your time with the erase option, because it is permanent and stops Find Hub from locating the phone afterward.
