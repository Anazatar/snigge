You do not always need a cable or satellite subscription to watch
television. Depending on where you live, you may be able to receive
local broadcast channels with an antenna or watch free programmes
through streaming apps.

These options work in different ways. An antenna receives television
signals through the air, while a streaming app uses your internet
connection. Both can provide plenty to watch, although channel choices
depend on your location.

This guide covers the basics for viewers in the United States and the
United Kingdom.

## 1. Understand the two main ways to watch free TV

**Over-the-air TV** uses an antenna to receive local broadcast signals.
In the US, this is often called antenna TV. In the UK, the main digital
terrestrial service is Freeview.

**Free streaming TV** uses an internet connection to deliver programmes.
Some services offer films and shows on demand, while others provide
scheduled channels that feel more like traditional television. These are
often supported by advertisements.

You can use either option or combine them. For example, an antenna can
provide local broadcast channels while a free streaming app offers
additional films and themed channels.

> **Good to know:** "Free" usually means there is no monthly TV
> subscription. You may still need an antenna, an internet connection, a
> compatible device, or---in the UK---a TV Licence for certain viewing.

## 2. Check whether your TV can receive antenna channels

Most modern televisions have a digital TV tuner built in. In the US, the
Federal Trade Commission says TV sets made since 2007 generally have a
digital tuner for receiving over-the-air broadcasts. Older televisions
may need a compatible digital converter box.

In the UK, many televisions include a Freeview receiver. To watch live
Freeview channels through the TV guide, you need a working aerial
connected to the television.

Look at the back of your TV for a socket labelled **Antenna**,
**Aerial**, **RF In**, or similar. If you are unsure, check the TV's
manual or search the manufacturer's support site using the model number.

## 3. Connect an antenna and scan for channels

If you already have an aerial or antenna connection in your home, check
that its cable is firmly connected to the TV's antenna input. If you are
using an indoor antenna, follow its instructions and place it where
reception is likely to be strongest.

Next, use your TV remote to open the settings menu. Look for a section
such as **Channels**, **Broadcast**, **Tuning**, or **Antenna Setup**.
Choose **Auto Scan**, **Auto Tuning**, **Channel Scan**, or a similar
option.

The television will search for available broadcast channels and save the
ones it can receive. The scan may take several minutes.

In the US, available channels depend on local broadcast coverage,
distance, buildings, terrain, and antenna placement. The FCC's reception
map can help you see which stations may be available in your area. In
the UK, Freeview's channel checker can show the channels expected for
your postcode.

## 4. Choose a suitable antenna

Indoor antennas are usually the simplest option to try because they can
sit near the television or a window. Reception varies, though, and an
indoor antenna may not work well in every building or location.

An outdoor or loft antenna may receive a more reliable signal in some
areas, but installation can be more involved. In the UK, Freeview
recommends a suitable wideband aerial for receiving the range of digital
TV signals. If an outdoor aerial needs installing or repairing, use a
qualified installer rather than attempting roof work yourself.

Be cautious of advertisements promising hundreds of channels or every
premium cable channel with one small antenna. An antenna can receive
broadcast stations within range; it does not unlock paid cable networks
or subscription services. The US FTC has warned consumers about
misleading antenna claims.

## 5. Try free streaming apps

If your TV connects to the internet, you may be able to install free
streaming apps from its app store. You can also watch on a phone,
tablet, computer, or a compatible streaming device connected to your TV.

Free services usually pay for programmes through advertising. Some
require a free account, while others let you start watching without
signing up. Check the app's official listing and terms in your country,
because availability and programmes can change.

Examples include:

-   **United States:** Tubi, Pluto TV, The Roku Channel, and PBS offer
    free content, although the selection and features vary by device and
    location.
-   **United Kingdom:** ITVX, Channel 4, 5, Pluto TV, and Tubi offer
    free, ad-supported viewing. BBC iPlayer is also available, subject
    to TV Licence rules.

Some smart TVs include their own free channel services, such as Samsung
TV Plus or LG Channels. These may already be installed, depending on the
television model and country.

> **Tip:** Start with the official app store on your TV. Avoid websites
> or apps that promise every paid channel for free, ask you to install
> unknown software, or request unusual payment details.

## 6. Install a streaming app on your TV

The menu differs between television brands, but the general process is
similar.

1.  Connect your TV to your home Wi-Fi network.
2.  Press the **Home** button on the TV remote.
3.  Open the television's **Apps**, **App Store**, or **Applications**
    section.
4.  Search for the name of a free streaming service.
5.  Select the official app and choose **Install** or **Download**, if
    needed.
6.  Open the app and follow its sign-in instructions, if an account is
    required.

If your television does not support the app, you may be able to use a
compatible streaming device or connect a laptop to the TV. Check
compatibility before purchasing extra equipment.

## 7. Know the UK TV Licence rules

If you are in the UK, check the TV Licence rules before watching. A TV
Licence is required to watch or record programmes as they are being
shown live on any channel or service. It is also required to watch any
BBC programme on BBC iPlayer, including catch-up programmes.

You generally do not need a TV Licence to watch on-demand programmes on
services such as ITVX, Channel 4, or 5, provided you are not watching
live television through them. The rules depend on what you watch, not
simply whether you use an aerial or an app.

Check the official GOV.UK guidance if you are unsure about your
household's situation. The licence rules are different from the US,
where there is no equivalent general TV licence.

## Troubleshooting: If free TV is not working

**The channel scan finds no stations.** Check that the antenna cable is
connected to the correct input and that the TV is set to scan for
antenna or aerial channels, not cable. Try repositioning an indoor
antenna and run the scan again.

**Some channels are missing or the picture breaks up.** Reception can
vary with location, weather, buildings, and antenna position. Check your
local coverage information and make sure the cable and antenna are in
good condition.

**The streaming app will not open.** Check your internet connection,
restart the TV, and see whether the app has an update. If the app is no
longer supported on your TV, check whether the service works on another
compatible device.

**The app asks you to sign in.** Some free services require an account.
Use the service's official app or website to create one, and read the
privacy information before providing personal details.

**You cannot find an app in your TV's store.** It may not be supported
on your TV model or in your country. Check the service's official
device-compatibility page rather than downloading an unofficial version.

**An antenna advertisement sounds too good to be true.** Be wary of
claims that a small antenna can provide every premium channel or
guarantee hundreds of stations anywhere. Reception depends on local
broadcast signals, and antennas do not replace paid services.

## Enjoy more TV without a monthly subscription

Antenna TV and free streaming apps can help you watch a variety of
programmes without paying for a traditional TV package. Start by
checking which broadcast channels are available where you live, then
explore a few legitimate free apps on your television.

Remember that channel selection differs by country, and free services
usually include adverts. If you are in the UK, check the TV Licence
rules for live viewing and BBC iPlayer before you watch.
