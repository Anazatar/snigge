If you are wondering how to update Mac software without risking your
files or interrupting your day, a little preparation makes the process
much easier. macOS updates bring security fixes, bug repairs, and
sometimes new features, but it is sensible to check a few things before
you begin.

You do not need to be an expert to update your Mac. Follow the steps
below, allow enough time for the installation, and keep your Mac
connected to power while it works.

## What you'll need

-   Your Mac and its administrator password, if your Mac asks for one.
-   A reliable internet connection.
-   Your Mac's power adapter, especially if you are using a MacBook.
-   A recent backup is strongly recommended before a major update.

## Step 1: Check which version of macOS you have

Before installing anything, it helps to know which version is currently
on your Mac. This can also help you understand whether an update is
available for your particular model.

1.  Click the **Apple menu** in the top-left corner of your screen.
2.  Choose **About This Mac**.
3.  Look for the macOS name and version number in the window that
    appears.
4.  Make a note of the information if you want to compare it with
    Apple's update information.

The version number tells you which release you are using. A smaller
update may fix problems or improve security, while a major upgrade moves
your Mac to a newer macOS version.

\[SCREENSHOT: About This Mac window showing the macOS name and version\]

> **Good to know:** Your Mac will not necessarily be offered every new
> macOS version. Apple only supports compatible Mac models, so the
> update options depend on the age and model of your computer.

## Step 2: Back up your Mac

A backup is a separate copy of your important files. It gives you a way
to recover them if something unexpected happens, so it is worth doing
before a major macOS upgrade.

Apple's built-in backup tool is called **Time Machine**. You normally
need an external storage drive for a Time Machine backup.

1.  Connect an external drive to your Mac.
2.  Open the **Apple menu** and choose **System Settings**.
3.  In the sidebar, click **General**.
4.  Select **Time Machine**.
5.  Follow the on-screen instructions to add a backup disk and start a
    backup, if one is not already set up.

The exact prompts can vary depending on your macOS version and whether
Time Machine has been used before. If you already use another trusted
backup service, check that it has completed a recent backup before
continuing.

\[SCREENSHOT: System Settings open to General \> Time Machine\]

> **Tip:** Do not assume that files stored in iCloud are a complete
> backup of your Mac. Check that important documents, photos, and other
> files are backed up in a way you can access if needed.

## Step 3: Get your Mac ready for the update

A few simple checks can prevent avoidable interruptions during
installation. You do not need to remove all your files or change
complicated settings.

1.  Save your work and close documents you are editing.
2.  Connect your MacBook to its power adapter. If you have a desktop
    Mac, make sure its power connection is secure.
3.  Check that your internet connection is working.
4.  Make sure you have enough free storage. Open **System Settings**,
    choose **General**, then select **Storage** to review the available
    space.
5.  If you use important work or specialist apps, check that their
    developers say they support the macOS version you plan to install.

If your Mac is nearly full, use the recommendations in **Storage** to
review large files and apps. Do not delete anything you are unsure
about. You can also move personal files to a suitable external drive
after confirming that you have another copy.

> **Careful:** Avoid starting an update just before an important
> meeting, trip, or deadline. The download and installation can take a
> while, and your Mac may restart more than once.

## Step 4: Find and install the macOS update

On recent versions of macOS, updates are managed in **Software Update**
inside System Settings. This is the normal place to check for Apple's
available updates.

1.  Click the **Apple menu**.
2.  Choose **System Settings**.
3.  Click **General** in the sidebar.
4.  Select **Software Update**.
5.  Wait while your Mac checks for available updates.
6.  If an update is offered, read its name and details.
7.  Click **Update Now** or **Upgrade Now**, depending on what is shown.
8.  Follow the on-screen instructions. Enter your administrator password
    if requested.

An **update** usually means a smaller release within your current macOS
version. An **upgrade** usually means moving to a newer major version.
Both are installed through Software Update when they are available for
your Mac.

\[SCREENSHOT: System Settings open to General \> Software Update,
showing an available update\]

> **Good to know:** You may see more than one update option. Read the
> descriptions before choosing, especially if one is a major upgrade and
> you would rather install only the latest security and maintenance
> update for your current version.

## Step 5: Let the installation finish

After you start the installation, your Mac may prepare files, restart,
and show an Apple logo or progress bar. This is normal.

1.  Keep your Mac connected to power.
2.  Leave it alone while the progress bar moves.
3.  Do not close the lid of your MacBook during the installation unless
    the on-screen instructions specifically tell you to.
4.  Wait for the Mac to restart and show the sign-in screen or desktop.
5.  Sign in and give the Mac a little time to finish setting things up.

The progress bar may appear to pause for a while. That does not
automatically mean the update has stopped. Avoid forcing your Mac to
shut down simply because the screen has not changed for a few minutes.

\[SCREENSHOT: Mac displaying the Apple logo and progress bar during a
macOS update\]

## Step 6: Check that the update worked

Once your Mac has restarted, you can confirm the installed version and
check that your everyday essentials still work.

1.  Open the **Apple menu**.
2.  Choose **About This Mac**.
3.  Check the macOS version shown.
4.  Open a few apps you use regularly.
5.  Check that your internet connection, printer, and any external
    accessories you rely on are working.

If you use an external monitor, printer, or other accessory, reconnect
it if necessary and check that it works as expected. Some apps may also
show their own updates after macOS has been updated.

## Troubleshooting: If the update does not work

Most updates complete without difficulty, but these checks can help if
you run into a problem.

-   **No update appears:** Your Mac may already be up to date, or it may
    not support the newest release. Check your Mac model and Apple's
    compatibility information.
-   **The download will not start:** Check your internet connection,
    then return to **System Settings \> General \> Software Update** and
    try again.
-   **There is not enough storage:** Open **System Settings \> General
    \> Storage** and review the recommendations. Keep a backup before
    removing personal files.
-   **The Mac seems stuck:** If the progress bar has not moved for a
    long time, keep the Mac connected to power. Check Apple's guidance
    for the specific message or screen you see before attempting a
    forced restart.
-   **An app no longer works as expected:** Check with the app's
    developer for an update or compatibility information.

If you cannot get the update to install, visit Apple's official support
site or contact Apple Support. If your Mac is managed by an employer or
school, its administrator may control when updates can be installed.

## Keep your Mac up to date

Updating macOS is a straightforward way to receive important fixes and
improvements. Back up first, connect to power, use Software Update in
System Settings, and give your Mac time to finish. With those simple
precautions, you can keep your Mac current with greater confidence.
